# Community Research Kit: Working With Deaf & Hard-of-Hearing People

Principle: **nothing about us without us.** Deaf people are paid advisors and co-designers, not test subjects.

## 1. Before you reach out

- Decide the target sign language first (ASL, ISL, BSL...). Each community is different.
- Budget to **pay** people: interviews (~$25-50/hr equivalent in your market), advisory board members (a retainer), and sign-language interpreters for any session where you are not fluent. Unpaid requests are a common reason products lose community trust.
- Offer every session in the person's preferred mode: sign (with a qualified interpreter or a Deaf facilitator), video chat with captions, or written/text chat. Never assume English or speech.
- Be honest from the first message: you are an early-stage builder, the tool is AI-assisted, and it may be wrong.

## 2. Where to find people

Start with organizations, not cold DMs to individuals.

| Region | Where to start |
|---|---|
| Global | World Federation of the Deaf (wfdeaf.org) and its national member associations |
| India | National Association of the Deaf (NAD India), Indian Sign Language Research & Training Centre (ISLRTC), local Deaf schools and associations |
| US | National Association of the Deaf (nad.org), local Deaf community centers, Gallaudet University / NTID communities |
| UK | British Deaf Association, RNID |
| Online | Deaf-led groups on Reddit (r/deaf), Facebook, Discord; Deaf YouTubers / ISL-ASL educators |
| Professionals | Registered sign-language interpreters (they know where current tools fail), Deaf educators, Deaf software engineers |

Verify current contact pages yourself; this list is a starting point, not a directory.

## 3. Outreach message (edit and send)

> Subject: Seeking Deaf advisors for an accessibility project (paid)
>
> Hi, I'm [Name]. I'm building a free tool with three parts: (1) helping Deaf and hearing people communicate in video calls such as Meet, Zoom and Teams, (2) live captions and sign output, and (3) a sign language learning space.
>
> I'm not Deaf and I don't want to build this without Deaf people leading the design. Before I write more code, I'd like to listen: what already works, what fails, and whether this tool should exist at all.
>
> I'm looking for a few paid advisors and interview participants. Sessions can be in sign (with an interpreter provided), video with captions, or text. Nothing you say will be shared without your consent, and you can stop at any time.
>
> Would you or someone in your community be open to a 45-minute conversation? Thank you for your time.
> [Name, contact, link to repo]

## 4. Interview guide (45 min)

Open with consent (section 6). Ask open questions; listen more than you pitch.

**Background**
1. Which language(s) do you use day to day (sign, written, speech)?
2. How do you join video calls at work, school or with family today?

**Pain points**
3. Tell me about the last video call that went badly. What happened?
4. What do auto-captions get wrong for you?
5. Have you used an interpreter in calls? What was good and bad about it?

**Existing tools**
6. Which apps or tools have you tried for sign translation or learning? What did you stop using, and why?

**Reactions to the idea** (show a sketch, not a pitch)
7. If a tool showed signing from speech in a call, what would make you trust it? What would make you delete it?
8. If a tool turned your signing into voice or subtitles, how accurate does it need to be before you'd use it? Who should confirm what it says?
9. When would you NOT want AI in the call (medical, legal, school)?
10. Should this ever replace a human interpreter? What is the line?

**Learning**
11. If hearing people learned from this tool, what must it teach correctly? What would worry you?
12. Who should be the teachers: avatars, recordings of Deaf signers, or both?

**Close**
13. What did I not ask that I should have?
14. Would you review the prototype later? (Paid, optional)

## 5. Advisory board (3-7 people)

- Majority Deaf or hard-of-hearing, ideally including Deaf people who are not tech-oriented.
- Include at least one qualified interpreter and one Deaf educator.
- Monthly review of designs and prototypes; they can veto features.
- Written charter: what they decide, how they are paid, how credit is given.

## 6. Consent (short form)

- I am being asked to take part in research about a sign-language communication tool.
- Participation is voluntary and paid at [rate]. I can stop at any time and still be paid for time given.
- Notes (and recordings, only if I agree) are stored securely and used only to improve this tool.
- My name and video are never shared without separate written permission.
- Any signing videos used to train AI require **separate, explicit, revocable** consent.

## 7. Testing plan

1. **Concept stage:** interviews + paper/Figma sketches.
2. **Prototype stage:** Deaf testers use captions + sign overlay in a real call; record failures, not just praise.
3. **Each release:** advisory board sign-off before shipping sign-to-speech features.
4. Track: accuracy, delay, trust, and "would you use this over what you have today?"

## 8. Design commitments to make public

- Always show live captions as a fallback.
- Label AI output as AI-assisted and possibly wrong.
- Never present the tool as a replacement for qualified interpreters in medical, legal or educational settings.
- Credit and pay Deaf contributors; give Deaf creators ownership/credit for sign content.
- Collect training data only with explicit consent.
